# Exploratory Data Analysis

## 1. Overview

Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) was performed on the cleaned retail transaction dataset to understand sales performance, profitability, customer segments, returns, discount patterns, regional performance, product performance, and trends over time.

The analysis was performed using Excel PivotTables and calculated metrics based on the `Orders_Cleaned` dataset.

The main objectives were to:

* Understand overall sales and profitability.
* Compare performance across product categories and subcategories.
* Examine the relationship between discounts and profitability.
* Analyze returned orders and their associated sales and profit.
* Compare customer segments and geographic regions.
* Analyze regional performance across product categories.
* Identify sales and profitability patterns over time.

---

## 2. Dataset Structure

The cleaned dataset contains **8,399 transaction rows**.

An important characteristic of the dataset is that `Order ID` is not unique. A single order can contain multiple transaction rows.

Therefore:

* Sales, profit, and quantity are analyzed at the transaction-row level.
* Order counts are calculated using unique `Order ID`.
* Return rate is calculated using unique orders rather than transaction rows.

### Key Dataset Metrics

| Metric                |          Value |
| --------------------- | -------------: |
| Transaction Rows      |          8,399 |
| Unique Orders         |          5,496 |
| Unique Customers      |            795 |
| Returned Orders       |            572 |
| Return Rate           |         10.41% |
| Total Sales           | $14,915,600.82 |
| Total Profit          |  $1,521,767.96 |
| Overall Profit Margin |         10.20% |
| Total Order Quantity  |        214,777 |

Overall profit margin is calculated as:

`Total Profit / Total Sales`

This is a weighted margin and is preferred over averaging individual transaction-level profit margins.

The analysis shows an association between discount levels and profitability, but it does not establish that discounts themselves caused the changes in profit.

# 11. Time-Series Analysis

Sales, profit, and quantity were analyzed by Order Year, Quarter, and Month.

## Annual Performance

| Year |         Sales |      Profit | Profit Margin |
| ---- | ------------: | ----------: | ------------: |
| 2009 | $4,209,139.46 | $434,538.79 |        10.32% |
| 2010 | $3,549,680.80 | $363,871.38 |        10.25% |
| 2011 | $3,436,816.70 | $381,455.99 |        11.10% |
| 2012 | $3,719,963.86 | $341,901.81 |         9.19% |

### Observations

* 2009 recorded the highest annual sales at approximately $4.21M.
* 2011 recorded the highest annual profit margin at approximately 11.10%.
* Sales declined from 2009 through 2011 and increased again in 2012.
* Profit increased from 2010 to 2011 despite lower sales.
* 2012 had higher sales than 2011 but lower profit and a lower aggregate margin.

This further demonstrates that sales growth and profitability do not necessarily move together.

## Quarterly Observations

Quarterly performance varied by year.

* 2009 had its highest quarterly sales in Q1.
* 2010 had its highest quarterly sales in Q4.
* 2011 had its highest quarterly sales in Q4.
* 2012 had its highest quarterly sales in Q1.

Therefore, the dataset does not show a single consistent highest-sales quarter across all years.

## Monthly Observations

Monthly results show substantial variation in both sales and profitability.

Examples include:

* January 2009: $516.3K sales and $62.3K profit.
* March 2010: $217.8K sales and only $1.1K profit.
* September 2010: $418.3K sales and $50.0K profit.
* November 2011: $367.8K sales and $39.6K profit.
* May 2012: $384.6K sales and $29.4K profit.

March 2010 is an example of a period where sales were relatively substantial but recorded profit was very low. This can be investigated further using category, discount, product, and regional dimensions if required.

# 12. Key EDA Findings

The completed EDA identified several important patterns:

1. **Sales and profitability are not proportional.**
   Higher sales do not necessarily result in higher profit margins.

2. **Furniture has substantially lower profitability than the other major categories.**
   Its overall margin was 2.27%, compared with 13.80% for Office Supplies and 14.81% for Technology.

3. **Furniture's profitability problem is concentrated in specific subcategories.**
   Tables and Bookcases generated combined negative profit of approximately $132.6K.

4. **Some high-sales subcategories have very low profitability.**
   Storage & Organization generated approximately $1.07M in sales but only $6.7K in profit.

5. **Discount levels are associated with changes in profitability.**
   Within the normal 0–10% range, higher discount bands generally correspond to lower margins, although the relationship is not perfectly monotonic.

6. **Discount effects differ across categories.**
   Furniture became slightly loss-making at the 6–10% discount band, while Office Supplies and Technology remained profitable.

7. **Returns represent a meaningful portion of the business.**
   572 of 5,496 unique orders were returned, giving an order-level return rate of 10.41%.

8. **Regional performance differs.**
   Central generated the largest sales, while South had a higher aggregate profit margin
